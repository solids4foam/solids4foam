**The evidence supports refinement trends and persistent benchmark differences, but several proposed error bounds are too strong. Ranked by importance:**

1. **Replace displacement “convergence” with measured refinement sensitivity.** You may claim that 2x′→4x′ changes \(u_y\) amplitude by 0.051 mm and \(u_x\) midpoint by 0.0131 mm (0.14%/0.4%). These are **not remaining-error bounds**. Ratios approaching or exceeding unity mean the spatial increment is insufficiently separated from other errors; they do not imply large absolute displacement errors. “Spatial order unresolved” is appropriate. The \(4/3\) temporal estimate assumes second-order asymptotic behaviour, which several triplets do not demonstrate. Short-window drift measures local stationarity, not distance from the eventual limit cycle.

2. **Correct the coupling-error interpretation.** The \(10^{-4}\)–\(10^{-5}\) difference is a sensitivity test, **not an upper bound** on the \(10^{-5}\) error, and a coarse-grid test does not establish fine-grid accuracy. Hitting iteration 30 does not necessarily mean failure: report actual tolerance violations and their residual magnitudes, particularly within measurement windows. Median residuals cannot characterize the tail. The 19.7% cap-hit rate also potentially contaminates the temporal triplet.

3. **Richardson/GCI: quote only as conditional estimates.** Frequency and lift amplitude show resolved, monotone refinement trends relative to the reported temporal sensitivities. Their apparent orders describe the **joint fluid–solid refinement path**, not either discretisation separately. Assuming a single asymptotic power law, your numbers give:
   
   - Frequency: extrapolated \(5.4609\) Hz; fine-grid GCI approximately **0.27%**.
   - Lift amplitude: extrapolated \(156.6\) N/m; fine-grid GCI approximately **6.1%**.

   These use \(r=2\), safety factor 1.25. Label them provisional, not certified uncertainty bounds; three monotone points alone do not establish asymptotics. [Richardson/GCI methodology](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/spatconv.html). Check stationarity of these QoIs themselves. Do not promote drag/lift-mean orders when competing sensitivities nearly equal their grid increments.

4. **Replay does not establish coupled displacement insensitivity.** The matched-time-step comparison strongly supports predominantly spatial 4x′→8x′ replay changes; the platform overlap supports reproducibility. However, **77–82 N/m is an observed spread, not a demonstrated few-percent error bound**. The earlier plateau did not establish convergence.

   Replay evaluates loading on a prescribed trajectory; coupled refinement changes trajectory, frequency, structural response and interface transfer. There is no coupled 8x′ result demonstrating response to that replay correction. Similar tip amplitudes can coexist with changing traction distributions and phase, or with cancellation between fluid and solid errors. Moreover, \(W\) rises from 0.042 to 0.077 J/m: phase and energy transfer remain sensitive. Solid sensitivity is substantial; its dominance on the finest pair remains unproven.

5. **Featflow: claim a persistent discrepancy in the tested solutions.** Your corrected finest result retains approximately +3.2% transverse amplitude and +6.6–6.7% streamwise midpoint-magnitude/amplitude differences. You may say the tested corrections and refinements **have not closed that gap**. You may not claim an established continuum discrepancy, an inherent FV/partitioning bias, or experimental validation.

   Match definitions carefully: benchmark “mean” is the extrema midpoint, including for forces. [Benchmark definitions](https://web.lums.edu.pk/~mudassar.razzaq/publications/TurekHronRazzaqWobkerSchaefer2010.pdf). Quantify the effect of 4 ms smoothing before comparing amplitudes; reconcile lift mean 5.03 versus reference 2.50 using identical definitions. The earlier tutorial sequence cannot establish the corrected sequence’s order.

Comparing against existing 2x′/4x-solid isolates solid refinement; comparing against 4x′/8x-solid isolates fluid refinement, subject to the coupling-error audit above. This directly tests cancellation and the proposed solid dominance. Preserve the common pre-6 s extraction, and extend stationarity sampling if affordable.
